## What this does

Play a clip and read the transcript. The audio is decoded in this tab, the
model runs in this tab, and the words appear **as they are decoded** rather
than all at once at the end.

**This page transcribes English only.** The model is multilingual on paper,
and the section below explains why the page does not pretend it is.

It is also the only experiment here that takes sound, which means it is the
one where "nothing leaves your browser" stops being an abstract claim. Upload
a voice memo and the recording stays on your machine — the usual way to get a
transcript is to send your voice to somebody else's computer.

## The model

Whisper is OpenAI's speech recognition model, trained on 680,000 hours of
audio collected from the web in 96 languages. `whisper-base` is the second
smallest of the family: 74 million parameters, 80 MB quantized.

It is an encoder-decoder, like the [translation](/experiments/translation)
model. The encoder reads a spectrogram of the audio; the decoder writes text
one token at a time, which is why the transcript fills in as it works.

## How it works

1. The audio is resampled to **16 kHz mono**, the only rate Whisper
   understands. The page does this with an `AudioContext` constructed at that
   rate, so the browser's own resampler does the work.
2. Those samples become a log-Mel spectrogram: a picture of which frequencies
   are present over time. That picture is what the encoder actually reads.
3. The decoder is given the language and the task, then writes tokens until
   it emits an end-of-transcript marker.

## English only, and told so

Whisper can detect the spoken language itself, and on a short clip it detects
badly. Handed a German sentence with nothing declared, this exact model
returned:

> It is the color of the black card and the green card.

Fluent English, confidently produced, with no relation to the audio. Told the
clip was German, the same model returned every word of it correctly. So the
page always declares the language rather than letting the model guess.

It declares English, and only English, because that is the only language this
size of Whisper is good at. A language selector was built and then removed:
`whisper-base` transcribes accented English well, and a four-second Spanish
sentence — *"el gato está sobre la tabla"* — came back as *"Fíjate a tua
estasurrala taula"*. Of the 680,000 hours Whisper was trained on, the large
majority is English, and the smaller checkpoints spend what capacity they have
on it.

Offering a choice of language would have been offering a setting with one good
value. Upload English audio and this works; upload anything else and it will
produce confident English nonsense.

## Limitations

**It needs clean speech.** Four clips were tested before the one above was
chosen. The Apollo 11 "one small step" broadcast returned "I'm going to stop
right now.", and the Apollo 13 transmission returned "I think we're going to
be in the middle of the day." Both are famous recordings, and both are noisy
1960s radio — far from the training distribution.

**Silence and noise produce invented text, not silence.** The decoder has to
emit something, so a clip with nothing in it tends to come back with a
plausible sentence. This is the same failure the generative models on this
site all share: there is no "I don't know" token.

**Model size shows up as accent robustness.** This page started on
`whisper-tiny`, 45 MB, which transcribes the Kennedy clip flawlessly. Handed a
recording of an ordinary sentence read by a non-native English speaker, it
returned:

> These are all the other ones inside your browser. You will never have to be
> in the computer.

The sentence read aloud was "This model runs inside your browser. The audio
never left your computer." The same recording on `whisper-base` came back
correct. That is the entire difference between the two models here: not the
famous clip, which both get right, but an ordinary voice, which only the
larger one does.

**Be suspicious of the Kennedy clip.** It is the demo file shipped with
Whisper itself, and it flatters the model twice over: a native speaker
recorded in a studio, reading one of the most quoted sentences in English.
Whisper's decoder is a language model, so a phrase it has effectively
memorised needs very little acoustic evidence to produce. Judge the model on
your own voice instead — the upload button is there for that.
